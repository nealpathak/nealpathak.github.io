Is the operating year under control, and what breaks first when something slips? The annual obligation cycle as a dependency chain — critical path, slack, hard regulatory dates, and the cascade when one deliverable runs late.

Annual obligation calendar

Offsets are days from the anchor date. Lead time is how long the obligation takes once it can start. An obligation cannot start before everything it depends on has finished — that is what makes the calendar a chain rather than a list.

Operating calendar

Scheduled window for each obligation. Outlined bars are the binding chain — the items carrying the least float, where a day lost is a day lost at the regulatory deadline.

Methodology & model simplifications
  • Each obligation has an earliest start driven by its anchor date and offset, and a finish driven by its lead time. Where an obligation depends on others it cannot start until the last of them finishes, so the schedule is resolved in dependency order rather than by calendar date alone.
  • Slack is computed empirically: the obligation is delayed a day at a time until a hard deadline breaches that was not already breaching, and the last delay that still cleared is its slack. The binding chain is the set of obligations sharing the minimum slack — on a real calendar almost nothing has literally zero float, so keying off the minimum is what identifies the chain that actually constrains the year. This is a forward pass, not a full critical-path method with distinct free and total float.
  • Hard obligations are ones with an external deadline — regulatory filings, premium tax, renewal binding. Soft obligations can move without a breach, which does not mean they can move without consequence.
  • Filing deadlines shift with the selected domicile, but only for the common annual items. Any real program should confirm dates against its own domicile's current requirements and its captive manager's calendar rather than these defaults.
  • Lead times are planning estimates, not commitments from the providers who owe the work. The point of the cascade view is to show which estimates the year actually depends on.
  • Excluded: multi-year regulatory examination cycles, board member availability, coverage-specific filings, intercompany funding mechanics, investment operations, and anything triggered by a large claim. A captive that is mid-crisis is not running this calendar.
  • A planning and governance instrument for seeing where the year is tight — not a project management system, and not a substitute for the captive manager's statutory calendar.
