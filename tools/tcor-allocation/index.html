Tool 06 · Owners and operating companies

Cost of Risk & Premium Allocation

Every owner of an operating company asks the captive the same two questions: what does risk actually cost my business, and why is my premium what it is. The first is answered by adding up what most reports leave out. The second is where the argument lives: how much of each company's own experience should count, and how far its premium is allowed to move in one year. Both knobs are on the page.

Allocation

Next year's premium by operating company

The bar is the proposed premium; the mark is what a pure exposure-based allocation would charge. An entity moves away from its exposure share only as far as its own claims history is credible, and never further than the cap.

Who has been paying for whom

Premium against losses over the experience window

A captive pools risk, so some subsidy is the point. The question owners ask is whether it is deliberate. This is each company's premium against the losses it generated, developed to ultimate, over the same window the allocation uses.

Cost of risk

Total cost of risk by operating company

Retained losses developed to ultimate, plus the excess and aggregate premium, the administrator's handling fees, and the captive's own running costs, allocated to the company that generated them. Expressed per unit of exposure so companies of different sizes can be compared.

Method

Assumptions on the surface

Entity ultimates
Each company's reported losses by line and policy year are developed with the line's factors from the loss development tool; green years use Bornhuetter–Ferguson with the company's exposure share of the priced expectation. Entity ultimates sum to the line ultimates.
Historical premium
Assumed to have been allocated by exposure share. That is what most captives do until someone builds this page.
Credibility
Classical: claims in the window divided by claims plus the constant. The constant is the number of claims at which a company's own experience gets half the weight. Actuaries argue about it; the slider lets the argument happen with the numbers visible.
Cap
Applied against the exposure-based figure, then renormalised so the line premium is fully allocated. A cap of zero reproduces the exposure allocation.
Not modelled
Large-loss capping inside the experience, schedule credits, and any deliberate cross-subsidy the owners have agreed. Each is a line of code once the policy is written.
Tests
node tools/tcor-allocation/selftest.mjs checks that allocations fully allocate the premium, respect the cap, collapse to exposure share at zero credibility, and follow relativity at full credibility.
