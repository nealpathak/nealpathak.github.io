Executive summary


      

    


  






  


    

      
Why this exists

      

        Most automation proposals fail one of two ways. They are argued on
        anecdote — this takes forever — with no number attached, or they
        are argued on a headline savings figure that no one can trace back to a
        step, a role, or an hour.
      

      

        Both failures have the same root cause: the current state was never costed
        properly. This model forces that first, then makes the automation case a
        consequence of it rather than a claim beside it.
      

      

        It also has to be able to say no. Load the low-volume intake sample and
        most of the build cases stop clearing their hurdle. That result is as
        useful as the other one.
      

    


    

      
How it works

      
    
        
  • 
          Expected passes. A step with rework rate x
          is executed 1 / (1 − x) times on average. A 25% rework rate
          means the step costs a third more than its touch time suggests. This is
          the single most common omission in a manual-process roll-up.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Loaded rates. Each role is priced at fully loaded annual
          cost over productive hours — not salary over 2,080. Understating the rate
          understates the prize.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Elapsed vs. touch. Cycle time is queue time plus expected
          touch time, weighted by how many instances reach each step. The step that
          costs the most and the step that delays the most are usually not the same
          step, and the model names both.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Capacity, not headcount. Savings are expressed as FTE
          released and as additional volume absorbable at current headcount. What
          an organisation does with released capacity is a decision, not an output.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Sensitivity. Each driver is moved on its own, both
          directions, against horizon net value. The ranking tells you which
          assumption to go verify before anyone signs anything.
        
    • 
      

    


    

      
What it assumes

      
    
        
  • 
          Automation removes a share of touch time. The residual manual share keeps
          its original rework rate — deliberately conservative, since automation
          usually suppresses rework too.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Elapsed time is additive. There is no modelling of contention, batching,
          shift boundaries, or parallel paths. For sequential back-office work this
          holds well enough; for anything with genuine queueing dynamics it does not.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          No discount rate is applied. Over 24–36 months at these margins the
          sequencing does not change, though absolute net would fall modestly.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Build and run costs are inputs, not estimates. They are the largest source
          of error in any case of this shape, which is exactly why they appear in the
          sensitivity chart.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Every dataset here is synthetic. Nothing is derived from any real book of
          business, claim, matter, or employer.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Illustrative modelling only — not actuarial, accounting, or legal advice.
