Why this exists

      

        A loss run tells you what has been reported. It does not tell you what the
        year will cost, whether the funding held against it is adequate, or how much
        of the aggregate is already spoken for. Those three questions drive
        reserving, funding, and renewal pricing, and they are all answered from the
        same triangle.
      

      

        The usual failure is not arithmetic. It is a single ultimate presented with
        no method beside it, no sense of how much of the number came from the factor
        rather than the data, and no statement of what would have to be true for it
        to be wrong.
      

      

        So this shows three methods at once, exposes the weight each carries, and
        runs a diagnostics pass whose job is to argue with the output. On the
        four-year sample it says, in effect, do not quote this yet.
      

    


    

      
How it works

      
    
        
  • 
          Chain ladder. Age-to-age factors from the triangle,
          selected by volume-weighted or simple average across all years or the
          latest three, times a tail. Ultimate is the latest cumulative times the
          cumulative factor. Any factor can be overridden by hand, and an override
          is coloured so nobody mistakes it for an average.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Bornhuetter-Ferguson. Reported to date plus the
          unreported share of an a priori ultimate. Stable on green years precisely
          because it ignores the noise in them.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Benktander. The credibility blend of the two, weighting
          chain ladder by the share already reported. It is the default because it
          resolves the argument the other two are having: lean on the data as the
          year matures, lean on the a priori while it is thin.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Erosion. Paid, case, and IBNR stacked against each year's
          own aggregate limit, with headroom stated in dollars — the number that
          matters is how much adverse development it takes to pierce, not the
          percentage.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Renewal indication. Ultimate loss ratios on years mature
          enough to carry weight, trended forward, against the a priori. Green years
          are excluded by design — a year that is 20% reported tells you nothing
          about its own loss ratio, and including it just imports the a priori back
          into the answer it was supposed to test.
        
    • 
      

    


    

      
What it assumes

      
    
        
  • 
          Development in the future resembles the average of the past. A change in
          case reserving philosophy, in claim mix, or in settlement pace breaks that
          assumption without breaking the arithmetic, which is what makes it
          dangerous.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          The tail factor is an input. Nothing beyond the end of the triangle
          supports it, and on the four-year sample it carries more of the answer
          than the observed data does — the diagnostics say so out loud.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Ultimates are undiscounted and gross of reinsurance and excess recoveries
          unless the triangle loaded was already net.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Erosion is aggregate-basis only. Applying a per-occurrence retention needs
          claim-level detail a triangle does not carry.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          A point estimate is the least useful form of this answer. Vary the tail,
          the selection method, and the a priori — the spread is the finding.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Every triangle here is synthetic, generated from a stated reporting pattern
          and a fixed perturbation. Nothing is derived from any real book of
          business, claim, or employer. Imported files are parsed in the browser and
          never leave it.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Illustrative modelling only — not an actuarial opinion, not a reserve
          certification, and not accounting or legal advice.
