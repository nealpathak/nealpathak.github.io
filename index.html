Decision instruments for the point where legal exposure meets capital allocation.

Most organizations run risk and legal as separate cost centers with separate ledgers. The decisions that carry real money live at the seam between them — how much risk to retain, when a case is worth settling, whether a captive earns its frictional cost, what a legal department should cost to run. The models below are built to answer those questions with numbers rather than instinct.

Approach

The work behind these tools runs across a captive professional liability program — capital adequacy, funding, and aggregate erosion measured against renewal pricing — alongside litigation and settlement strategy, contract and claims operations, and the reporting that carries all of it to a board. What is published here is the generalized form of questions that arrived with real numbers attached.
