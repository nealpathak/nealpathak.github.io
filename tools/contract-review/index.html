Contract Playbook Checker

Read a draft against a written negotiation playbook: what deviates and by how much, what is missing, who has to approve it, and the language to send back — with the text each finding relies on shown next to it.

Document

Fewer than half the playbook clauses were located. Either this draft is unusually thin or this is the wrong playbook for the document type. Check the fit before relying on anything below.

Playbook in force

Thresholds and severities are editable in the table below the findings. Export it as JSON and the same positions can drive a checklist, an intake form, or someone else's review queue.

What this is not

Deterministic pattern matching, not comprehension. It finds what the playbook told it to look for. A clause drafted in unusual language will be missed entirely, and silence from a check is not a good clause — it is an unread one.

Why this exists

      

        Most organisations have a negotiation playbook. It usually lives in a
        partner's head, a slide deck from two reorganisations ago, or a Word
        document nobody opens. The positions are real; the artefact is not.
      

      

        Writing the playbook down as structured data changes what it can do. The
        same file that drives this checker can drive an intake questionnaire, a
        training module, a self-service approval matrix, or an audit of what was
        actually signed last year. That is the difference between a document and a
        control.
      

      

        The review output is the secondary benefit. The primary one is that the
        positions now exist somewhere they can be versioned, argued with, and
        handed to someone else.
      

    


    

      
How it works

      
    
        
  • 
          Segmentation. The draft is split on numbered headings,
          article and section markers, and bare capitalised headings, falling back
          to paragraph blocks when a document carries no numbering.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Classification. Each segment is scored against every
          clause type by weighted term matching, with terms in a heading counting
          two and a half times — a heading is the drafter telling you what the
          clause is. The matched terms are shown in the coverage map so a wrong
          label is visible rather than silent.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Checks. Four kinds: a phrase that must be present, a
          phrase that must be absent, and numeric thresholds in either direction
          extracted from the surrounding sentence. Every finding carries the text it
          relied on and the position in the document, so it can be verified in
          seconds rather than taken on trust.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Grading and routing. Findings are weighted ten, four and
          one by severity. The worst severity present sets the approval route, so
          the tool answers who signs this rather than only what is
          wrong with it.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Fallbacks. Each check carries the standard position, the
          fallback the organisation will actually accept, and drafted language. A
          finding without a proposed remedy is just a complaint.
        
    • 
      

    


    

      
What it assumes

      
    
        
  • 
          This is deterministic pattern matching, not comprehension. It finds what
          it was told to look for and nothing else. No model reads the contract, and
          nothing here infers meaning from context.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          A clause drafted in unusual language will not be located, and its checks
          will silently not run. That is why the coverage map exists and why it sits
          above the findings rather than below them.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Each segment maps to at most one clause type. Where a draft combines term
          and termination in a single section, one of the two will report as not
          found.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Numeric thresholds are read from the sentence around them. A figure that
          lives in a schedule, an exhibit, or a table will not be seen.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Absence of a finding is not a clean bill of health. It means no check
          fired — which could mean the clause is fine, or that no check covers the
          thing that is wrong with it.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          The playbook shipped here is a plausible generic position, not anyone's
          actual policy. Every sample contract is invented. Nothing is derived from
          any real agreement, counterparty, client, or employer.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          This is a triage aid that tells a reviewer where to look first. It is not
          a legal review, it does not replace one, and it is not legal advice.
