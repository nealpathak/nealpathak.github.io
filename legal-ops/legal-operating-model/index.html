Methodology & model simplifications
  • The in-house effective rate is loaded cost divided by productive hours, then grossed up by management overhead. Loaded cost should include salary, bonus, benefits, and allocated occupancy and technology — not base salary alone, which is the most common way this comparison gets rigged in favor of insourcing.
  • Work is insourced where the in-house effective rate beats the outside rate and the resulting demand supports at least half an attorney at the stated utilization. Matter types that pass on rate but fail on volume are flagged sub-scale rather than silently insourced — the half-FTE nobody can hire is where these models usually leak.
  • Headcount is rounded up to the nearest half attorney per matter type, then summed. Capacity is not pooled across matter types; in practice some of it would be, which makes the headcount estimate modestly conservative.
  • Panel consolidation applies a flat rate discount at the target tier to all remaining outside spend. Real consolidation delivers rate concessions unevenly, and the leverage depends on how much volume each firm stands to gain or lose.
  • Excluded: severance and ramp time on any transition, recruiting cost, the litigation surge risk that makes a thin internal team expensive, technology spend, and the quality difference between internal generalists and specialist outside counsel — which is a judgment call the model cannot price.
  • A planning instrument for sizing the conversation, not a headcount business case on its own.
