Methodology & model simplifications
  • Funded premium is set to expected loss cost plus captive expense load and trends at the loss cost trend. Commercial premium trends separately at the market rate trend — set the two equal to neutralize any rate-cycle advantage.
  • The economic comparison counts the parent's actual cost: incurred losses, frictional expenses (fronting, premium tax, operating cost), formation cost, and the opportunity cost of capital posted to the captive, less investment income earned on capital and held funds. Premium paid into a wholly owned captive is an intercompany transfer and is excluded from both sides.
  • Losses are deterministic at the stated loss ratio — no stochastic frequency or severity, and no reserve development beyond the selected payout pattern. The adverse scenario is a flat 20-point loss ratio load, not a modeled tail.
  • Payout patterns are generic short, medium, and long-tail shapes, not company triangles. Reserves are cumulative incurred less cumulative paid.
  • Investment income is credited on average invested assets at a flat yield; no asset-liability matching, duration, or mark-to-market.
  • Excluded and left to diligence: domicile selection and its regulatory capital rules, tax treatment and risk-distribution qualification, reinsurance and stop-loss structuring, collateral negotiation with the fronting carrier, and audit, actuarial, and management fees beyond the single operating-cost line.
  • This is a screening instrument for whether a captive merits a formal feasibility study — it is not one.
