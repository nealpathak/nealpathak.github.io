Methodology & model simplifications
  • Annual claim count is Poisson at the stated frequency; individual severities are lognormal at the stated mean and coefficient of variation. Ten thousand simulated years per retention level, driven by a fixed seed so the same inputs always return the same figures.
  • Retained loss per year is the sum of each claim capped at the per-occurrence retention. There is no aggregate stop-loss — adding one would compress the tail materially and is the first structural change worth pricing after this screen.
  • Guaranteed cost is expected ground-up losses multiplied by the market load. Excess premium above a retention is ceded expected losses multiplied by the excess load. Both are single-year, single-line, and ignore experience rating and the market cycle.
  • Retained programs carry claims administration per claim, collateral posted as a share of expected retained losses at a letter-of-credit fee, and a capital charge on the difference between the tail outcome and the expected outcome — the buffer the balance sheet has to stand behind.
  • The captive path adds operating cost and a frictional load on funded premium, and credits investment income on funds held. It does not model domicile capital rules, tax treatment, or risk distribution.
  • The captive is compared on cost alone, and on cost alone a single line at modest volume often loses to a plain self-insured retention. The reasons captives get formed anyway — reserve deductibility, direct access to reinsurance, multi-line diversification credit, and the funding discipline of a real balance sheet — sit outside this comparison. Read a narrow captive loss here as "not on these economics," not as "not worth doing."
  • One line of coverage in isolation. Correlation across lines, and the diversification credit a multi-line captive earns, are outside the model.
  • A screening instrument for framing the retention conversation with a broker or actuary, not a substitute for either.
